When the central heating isn't enough or the heating season hasn't started yet, the choice usually comes down to two types of electric heater: an oil-filled radiator and a fan heater. Both turn electricity into heat with practically the same efficiency — what differs is how they give it off. That decides which one suits you better.

## How they work

**A fan heater** has a heating element and a fan that blows air through it. You feel the warmth within seconds, but once it is switched off the room cools down quickly. The fan is audible.

**An oil-filled radiator** has a heating element immersed in oil sealed inside the fins. The oil takes about a quarter of an hour to heat up and then gives off warmth across the whole surface of the fins — gently and evenly. After switching off it keeps radiating heat for a long time. With no fan, it is practically silent.

## Which to choose

| | Oil-filled radiator | Fan heater |
|---|---|---|
| Heating up | slower, about a quarter of an hour | instant |
| Warmth after switching off | long | short |
| Noise | practically silent | fan audible |
| Best for | keeping a room warm for hours | quick, short bursts of heat |

**Choose an oil-filled radiator** if you want to top up the heat in a bedroom, office or child's room for hours — at night too, because it won't disturb you. **Choose a fan heater** if you want to quickly warm the bathroom before a shower or the garage for a quarter of an hour.

## What it costs

A 1200 W appliance at full power uses 1.2 kWh per hour — whether it's oil or a fan. You can calculate the cost yourself: **power × running time × price per kWh** from your electricity bill. At an example price of €0.35 per kWh, an hour at full power costs about €0.42.

In practice, an appliance with a thermostat runs at full power only until the room reaches the set temperature — after that the element switches on only occasionally, so the actual cost is lower. Closed doors help, as does a free-standing heater with no furniture or curtains in front of it.

## The right power

For supplementary heating, a rough rule is **about 100 W per m²** at a typical ceiling height:

- [OW 7832](/en/produkty/ow-7832) — 800 W, 7 fins: small bedroom, bathroom, study up to about 8 m²,
- [OW 7833](/en/produkty/ow-7833) — 1000 W, 9 fins: rooms of about 10 m²,
- [OW 7834](/en/produkty/ow-7834) — 1200 W, 11 fins: rooms of about 12 m² or poorly insulated spaces.

In older buildings, under the roof or with large windows, go one size up. The thermostat will switch off anyway once the room is warm.

## Safety

All three oil-filled radiators have a continuously adjustable thermostat, overheat protection, a power indicator light and a steel housing on stable feet. Place the radiator away from curtains, bedding and furniture, never dry clothes on it and don't put it where it can easily be knocked over.
