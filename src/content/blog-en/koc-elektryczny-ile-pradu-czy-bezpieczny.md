On cold nights many people would rather warm just the bed than turn up the heating in the whole flat. An electric blanket does it quickly and cheaply — but before buying, two questions usually come up: how much does it cost to run, and can you sleep on it safely? The first can be answered to the cent.

## How much electricity does an electric blanket use

The energy use of any appliance is calculated the same way: **power × running time**. The power is printed on the rating label or in the specifications.

Take the [OW 7449](/en/produkty/ow-7449) heated underblanket, rated at 60 W:

- 1 hour at full power: 60 W × 1 h = **0.06 kWh**,
- a whole night (8 hours): 60 W × 8 h = **0.48 kWh**,
- a month at 8 hours a day: about **14.4 kWh**.

To convert this into money, multiply the result by the price per kWh on your electricity bill. At an example price of €0.35 per kWh, a whole night costs at most about 17 cents and a month about €5.

That is the upper limit. On lower settings the heating element does not run continuously, so actual use is lower. You save the most by pre-warming the bed on a higher setting before you go to sleep and then lowering the temperature or setting the timer for the night — the OW 7449 has a 1–10 hour timer.

For comparison: the [OW 7450](/en/produkty/ow-7450) heating pad is rated at 100 W, but it is usually used for shorter, targeted sessions — an hour or two for the back or neck.

## Is an electric blanket safe

Modern electric blankets are designed to be used against the body, including at night. Two things matter when buying:

- **Overheat protection** — switches the heating off if the temperature rises above normal. Both of our models have it.
- **Timer** — the blanket switches itself off even if you fall asleep with the heating on.

Safety also depends on how you use it. The most important rules:

- **Use the blanket spread flat.** Do not fold it, roll it up or put heavy objects on it while it is on.
- **Do not use it when wet.** After washing, the blanket must be completely dry before you reconnect the cable.
- **Check the cable and plug.** If there is visible damage, stop using the blanket.
- **Do not stick pins or safety pins into it.**
- **It is not for everyone.** Not for infants, young children or people who cannot feel overheating or cannot switch the device off themselves.

The exact rules for each model are always in the user manual — for our blankets you can download it from the product page.

## Washing and storage

Blankets and pads with a detachable cable can go in the washing machine: the OW 7449 at up to 30 °C, the OW 7450 at up to 40 °C. After the season, store the blanket loosely folded, without pressure and dry — sharp creases damage the heating wires.

## Summary

An electric blanket is one of the cheapest sources of warmth for the night: a 60 W blanket uses less than half a kilowatt-hour over a whole night. Choose a model with overheat protection and a timer, use it spread flat and according to the manual — and you won't have to heat the whole bedroom.
