Cutting your hair at home saves time and money, but one wrong guard can undo an effect you've been working toward for months. Unlike at the barber, you don't get a same-day do-over — so it's worth understanding how the length-adjustment system actually works before you switch the clipper on.

## The lever and the guards are not the same thing

Most clippers have two independent ways of adjusting length, which are easy to mix up:

- The **lever** changes the gap between the clipper's own blades, with no guard attached — this gives the shortest, most precise cuts, usually in the 0.8–2 mm range. It's used for contouring, lines and very short styles.
- **Guide combs** fit over the blade and extend the cut by a few millimetres to over a centimetre — these are the "numbers" you know from barbershops (e.g. a 6 mm guard is the popular "number 6").

Combining both mechanisms gives you one continuous length range instead of a handful of fixed options — that combination is exactly what lets the same clipper shave stubble down to zero and leave 13 mm on top in the same session.

## Which setting for which style

- **0.8–1.2 mm (lever only, no guard)** — contours, side lines, very short stubble.
- **3–6 mm (shortest guards)** — a classic "short" haircut, the sides of a fade.
- **10–13 mm (longest guards)** — the top of the head on styles with a length contrast, longer but still tidy stubble.

If you're not sure, always start with a longer guard. You can shorten hair at any point — you can't lengthen it back.

## Common mistakes when cutting hair at home

- **Cutting against the grain on the first pass** instead of with it — start by running the clipper in the direction hair grows; only later passes against the grain are for evening things out.
- **Moving too fast** — the clipper needs a moment of contact with the hair for the blade to cut it evenly, not just brush past it.
- **A dirty or dull blade** — hair starts catching and tugging instead of being cut cleanly, which you'll feel and hear immediately.
- **Pressing too hard** — the clipper is meant to glide over the skin on its own; forcing it down increases the risk of uneven patches.
